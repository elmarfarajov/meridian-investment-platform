# Building a benchmark that attribution can use

Attribution is only as good as the benchmark's own decomposition. A benchmark that is
just a return series can say *how much* the portfolio beat it by, but not *where*. For
that, the benchmark must be built the way an index provider builds one: constituents
with weights, sectors, regions and currencies, day by day.

**Implementation:** [`performance/benchmark.py`](../../src/meridian/performance/benchmark.py),
[`services/demo_performance.py`](../../src/meridian/services/demo_performance.py),
[`marketdata/providers/synthetic.py`](../../src/meridian/marketdata/providers/synthetic.py) ·
**Decision:** [ADR 0022](../adr/0022-a-synthetic-benchmark-from-the-same-market.md)

## 1. The policy benchmark

The account's mandate is written as a policy benchmark:

| Sleeve | Weight | Measured by |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Equity | 80% | Meridian World Equity |
| Fixed income | 15% | The US Treasury 2032 the account can hold, total return |
| Cash | 5% | Zero (the account's cash earns nothing) |

The sleeves are **rebalanced to policy on the first valuation day of each month** and
drift with their returns in between, as a real mandate's benchmark does. The bond
sleeve's total return is the change in its dirty price plus coupons, computed from the
Day 1 bond mathematics on the Day 2 clean prices.

## 2. Meridian World Equity

A synthetic, capitalisation-weighted, total-return index of 36 stocks in five regions
and eight sectors:

| Region | Stocks |
| --- | ---: |
| North America | 19 |
| Europe ex UK | 6 |
| United Kingdom | 4 |
| Japan | 4 |
| Switzerland | 3 |

**Six** constituents are the demonstration book's own single stocks, whose prices come
from the Day 2 market. The other **thirty** are companions with plainly synthetic
identifiers (`BM-US-FIN-1`, `BM-JP-CD-1`). They stand for the kinds of company a world
index holds (a large bank, an oil major, a Japanese carmaker) without claiming to be
any real one. Starting capitalisations are chosen so the regional mix looks like a
world index: roughly three quarters North America.

Weights **drift with total return**, dividends reinvested, as a cap-weighted index's
do. Each constituent's daily return is split into a local total return and the
currency move on the closing local value. This is the portfolio's own convention
([ADR 0017](../adr/0017-the-value-bridge-is-exact.md)), so the currency effect in the
attribution compares like with like.

## 3. Companions from the same market

The companions must move with the book the way real stocks move with each other.
Otherwise the benchmark would be noise, and every active return would look like skill
or bad luck. `SyntheticMarket.companion` generates new instruments inside the **same**
synthetic market. It replays the market's own random draws for the market factor and
the sector factors, so a companion in the technology sector shares Apple's and
Microsoft's sector shocks. Only its idiosyncratic noise comes from a separate random
stream (`seed + 1000`).

Two properties follow, and both are tested:

- the market factor seen by the companions is identical to the book's;
- generating companions does not change a single price of the existing instruments,
  so the Day 2 and Day 3 numbers are untouched.

Over the period the equity index returned -12.5% and the policy benchmark -8.3%. The
book and its benchmark have a correlation of 0.87 and a beta of 0.85, which is
realistic for an active global equity mandate.

## 4. Segments

A held equity's sector comes from the security master and a constituent's from the
index definition, both with GICS-style names. The region comes from the country: the
United States maps to North America; Germany, France and the Netherlands to Europe ex
UK; the UK, Switzerland and Japan stand alone. Index funds have no segment of their
own: they are looked through (see [the attribution note](performance-attribution.md)). The bond sleeve is its own segment, "Fixed income", and cash is "Cash", in both
dimensions. So the policy's 15/5 split is measured against the account's actual bond
and cash weights, not buried in the equity segments.

## 5. Why not a real index?

Real index constituents and weights are licensed data. A synthetic index built inside
the same market as the book keeps the whole platform reproducible from one seed. Every
number in the documentation can be regenerated by anyone with the repository, and the
attribution tests can assert exact values. The interfaces (`EquityIndex`,
`PolicyBenchmark`, `BenchmarkDay`) take constituents and total-return series. A real
index file would plug in without changing the attribution code.
